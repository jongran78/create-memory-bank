About the Project

What remains
when memory fades?

Memory Bank is a digital archive of human memories. It collects fragments of experiences, images, stories, sounds, objects, and moments that people wish to preserve. Not everything is complete. Not everything is certain. Some things are remembered differently. Some things are almost forgotten.

01 / Purpose

A collection of what remains.

We remember places that no longer exist, people we have lost, ordinary moments that somehow stayed with us, and experiences that become less clear with time.

Memory Bank creates a space for these fragments. A photograph, a sentence, a voice recording, a description of an object, or a memory without a beginning or end can become part of the archive.

The goal is not to preserve perfect stories. It is to preserve traces of human experience.

02 / Memory

Memory is not a recording.

We do not remember the past exactly as it happened. Memories change. Details disappear. Images influence what we believe we remember. Stories told by others become part of our own recollections.

Two people can experience the same event and remember entirely different things. Both memories may feel real. Neither may contain the complete story.

Memory Bank does not require certainty. A memory can be incomplete, contradictory, uncertain, or even difficult to explain.

03 / Images

Images preserve and distort.

Photographs are often treated as evidence of the past. They can help us remember people, places, and events. But an image also has limits. It captures one moment, from one perspective, chosen by someone.

A photograph may preserve a face while the memory of the person fades. It may document a place that no longer exists. It may become the only surviving representation of an experience.

What happens when we remember the photograph more clearly than the event itself?

04 / Power

Every archive makes choices.

An archive is never completely neutral. Someone decides what can be collected, how it is categorized, what can be searched, what becomes visible, and what remains hidden.

Memory Bank asks who controls the preservation of experience. Which memories become part of a collective record? Which memories remain unseen? What happens when a system organizes human experience into categories?

Even an attempt to preserve everything requires choices about what preservation means.

05 / Technology

Can technology preserve what disappears?

Digital technology allows memories to be stored, copied, shared, searched, and connected across time and distance. A fragment of someone's life may survive long after the person, place, or event is gone.

But preserving a file is not the same as preserving an experience. Data can survive while meaning changes. Context can disappear. Platforms can vanish. Information can become inaccessible.

Memory Bank explores both the possibility of digital preservation and its limitations.

We don't remember things exactly as they happened. We remember what remains.

06 / Invitation

Leave something behind.

You do not need to tell your entire story. You do not need to remember every detail. You do not need to explain why something matters.

Leave a fragment. A place. A sound. A face. Something ordinary. Something you are afraid of forgetting.

What remains is enough.
